Story of the Fisherman's Sashimi

Often referred to as the "Ternate Sashimi," Gohu Ikan is a testament to the raw, untamed freshness of the Maluku seas. Historically, this dish was born on the swaying decks of traditional fishing boats. Fishermen, spending days at sea, would take their freshest catch—usually Skipjack or Yellowfin Tuna—and prepare it immediately without any cooking heat.

Unlike Japanese Sashimi, Gohu Ikan is a complex balance of textures and flavors. It combines the buttery richness of raw tuna with the citric snap of calamansi lime, the earthy crunch of roasted kenari nuts, and the aromatic punch of fresh basil. The addition of hot coconut oil poured over the mixture provides a subtle sear and a silky finish that binds the ingredients together.
